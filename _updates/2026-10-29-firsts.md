I got a reminder that it’s time to renew the LLC. Which means it’s been about two years since I set up a company to try to buy a bar. And probably another year of trying to figure things out before that.

Almost three years of working on opening a place, and it’s still not open.

But lately I’ve been thinking about all the things I’m excited to do for the first time and how quickly they are approaching.

Placing our first booze orders—knowing that the government has now allowed me to buy and serve booze.

The first sale. Mostly because it means we’re open. We’ve flipped the sign, somebody has walked in and ordered something, and we can finally serve them.

Serving the first drink.

Putting down the first check. Hoping they had a good time.

Friends and Family...

The first stranger who comes through the doors. Someone who doesn’t know me or anyone involved, who just saw a bar and decided to come in. Starting the process of getting to know who will stumble into my bar.

The first time that person comes back because they feel like it’s a space they want to be in.

The first time a friend I haven’t seen in years walks in, standing in this place I’ve been telling them about for so long. Like an opportunity to introduce an old friend to a new friend.

The smell of food coming out of the kitchen.

Hearing the sound system in its full glory. Built by my college bud Joe D from Chicago. It’s gonna slap.

The first sounds of live music.

Getting to hire some of my favorite bands. There are so many musicians I love, and getting to hear each of them in this room for the first time is going to break my heart.

Opening night.

The first holiday at the bar. The second holiday at the bar. The third holiday at the bar. A slow one where only the locals are there.

I’m excited for things to work without needing my active energy every second. For people to be doing their thing, taking care of each other, and for me to get to sit there for a minute and watch it all happen.

I keep coming back to this one moment I’m waiting for. I want to sit in the back of the room, in the shadows, and watch people enjoy music. Watch people come together. Watch people forget about their phones for a while because there’s a great performance happening right in front of them. So caught up in it, they don’t even think about taking a video. Those moments are few and far between, but they’re worth hours and hours of work to create. I hope we get some.

And I’m excited for the first night I get to close up after a long day and know the place did its job. That the space served the people I wanted it to serve.

That’s been my North Star through all of this. All the work, all the energy, all the time. That’s the thing I keep picturing.

We’re still not open. There’s still plenty to do. But I can feel the firsts on the horizon.

And I’m really excited. And I hope you are too.
